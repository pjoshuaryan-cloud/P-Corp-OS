# Joshx — Post-Shoot Editing SOP (Version 1.1)

**Status:** Active. Use this on every paid shoot from now on, starting with Herbish. Exists to kill the recurring pattern of editing the night before *and* the morning of delivery under close-of-business pressure.

**Date:** 2026-08-29 (v1.0) — **Updated 2026-08-29 (v1.1):** added three concrete turnaround-tier playbooks below the quoting rule, so "how fast" has a repeatable day-by-day system instead of being improvised per job.

---

## The problem this fixes

The pattern: shoot day happens, footage sits until deadline pressure forces an edit session, that session runs into the night, and it repeats the morning of delivery. Root cause isn't editing speed — it's that (1) footage isn't secured/organized same-day so triage starts late and confused, and (2) turnaround is quoted before the actual edit workload is estimated, so there's no buffer if something takes longer than hoped. This SOP fixes both: a fixed same-day ingest habit, and a stage-by-stage time model you run *before* you commit to a delivery date.

---

## The workflow, in order

### Stage 0 — Same-day ingest & backup (shoot day, evening — non-negotiable)
**Time: 30–45 min.** This is the only editing-adjacent task allowed on shoot night. Not selects, not cutting — just:
1. Copy footage off cards to two locations (working drive + backup drive/cloud). Don't format cards until this is confirmed.
2. Rename/organize into project folder structure (raw footage / project files / deliverables — Design Agent will suggest structure via the `add_project` automation for Alpha Mode jobs; for Joshx jobs, replicate the same pattern manually: `ClientName_Project/01_Raw/02_Project/03_Deliverables`).
3. Quick scan for corrupted files or missed shots — flag anything that needs a reshoot *now*, while you're still near the location, not three days later.
4. Close the laptop. This is the stopping point, every time, no exceptions.

### Stage 1 — Triage / selects pass (next day, not shoot day)
**Time: 1–2 hrs** for a single short deliverable (≤60 sec); scale up for multiple deliverables or longer cuts.
Watch through, mark best takes/angles, discard obviously unusable footage. Output: a shortlist of clips, not a cut. This is a rest-day-adjacent task — do it once you're not running on shoot-day fatigue.

### Stage 2 — Rough cut
**Time: 2–4 hrs** per deliverable (30–60 sec final length; add ~1.5–2x for anything over 90 sec or requiring motion graphics/VO sync).
Structure and pacing only. No color grade, no polished audio mix. Goal: something watchable end-to-end.

### Stage 3 — Refine (color, audio, pacing tighten)
**Time: 1–3 hrs** per deliverable.
Color grade, audio levels/music sync, tightening cuts, titles/lower-thirds if needed.

### Stage 4 — Client review + revision buffer
**Time: build in 24–48 hrs for client feedback, then 1–2 hrs revision editing.**
This is the stage most people forget to quote for. Even a low-maintenance client needs a review window, and almost every job gets at least one revision request. Don't schedule delivery as if this stage doesn't exist.

### Stage 5 — Final export & delivery
**Time: 30–60 min** per deliverable (export, format for platform, upload/send, invoice trigger if final deliverable).

---

## Total realistic time, single 30–60 sec deliverable, one revision round

Ingest (0.5h) + Triage (1.5h) + Rough cut (3h) + Refine (2h) + Revision edit (1.5h) + Export (0.5h) ≈ **9 focused hours**, plus a mandatory 24–48h client review gap sitting inside the calendar timeline (not editing hours, but calendar days).

At a realistic solo pace of ~3–4 focused edit hours/day around other work (shoots, admin, other client jobs) — **not** 9 hours in one sitting — that's **2–3 working days of actual editing**, spread across a calendar window of **4–5 business days** once the review gap is included.

---

## Buffer rule: shoot day is never edit-all-night day

- **Shoot day = ingest only** (Stage 0). No triage, no cutting, same day.
- **Day after shoot = rest or triage, not rough cut.** If the shoot was a full day (like Malondie SS26 or Herbish, both full-day calls), the day after is triage at most — don't stack a heavy edit session on top of a heavy shoot day.
- Build the revision-review window (Stage 4) into the calendar as a real gap, not overlap with other edit work. Feedback often arrives when you're mid-way through something else — plan for that collision.

---

## Quoting rule of thumb — use this *before* you commit to a delivery date

**Minimum floor for any single short deliverable (≤60 sec), no revisions assumed yet: 3 business days from shoot day.**

Then adjust:
- **+1 business day** per additional deliverable in the same job (e.g., hero video + product video = base 3 + 1 = 4 business days minimum).
- **+2 business days** if the client is known to be slow/particular about feedback, or if this is a first-time client with unknown review speed.
- **+1 business day** if the footage volume is heavy (multiple locations, long shoot day, lots of B-roll to sort) — triage alone eats more time.
- Round the total **up**, not down, and quote that number to the client as the delivery date, not as a target you're privately padding.

**Applied to current jobs:**
- Herbish hero + product video (shot 2026-08-29, due 2026-09-01/Tuesday): base 3 + 1 (two deliverables) = 4 business days minimum → the Tuesday deadline is tight but roughly at the floor, not comfortably above it. No slack for revisions — flag this to the client now if a revision round is likely.
- Herbish factory machinery edit (30 sec, no firm deadline yet): quote **3 business days minimum** from today once a deadline is discussed, not "end of week" reflexively.
- Malondie SS26 (full-day shoot, now in post-production, R7,000 confirmed): treat as a heavier job — likely multiple deliverables — apply the +1 day per deliverable and +1 day for heavy footage volume rules before agreeing a delivery date.

**The actual habit to build:** don't answer "when can you deliver this" in the moment on-set or in a client chat. Say "let me check my post-production load and confirm a date" — buy yourself the 5 minutes to run this formula instead of guessing optimistically.

## Turnaround tier playbooks

The quoting formula above tells you the *floor*. These three playbooks tell you *what you actually do, day by day*, once you know which tier you're in. Pick the tier at quoting time, not mid-edit — switching tiers halfway through a job is how corners get cut by accident instead of on purpose.

### Tier 1 — Standard (5–7 business days)

This is the default. Use it whenever there's no reason to compress — most Alpha Mode content jobs, most first-pass client work, anything without a hard external deadline (an event date, an ad flight date, a launch).

- **Day 1 (shoot day):** Stage 0 only — ingest & backup, 30–45 min, evening. Nothing else. Grab the BTS 60 sec while you're at it (see shoot-day checklist) — it costs nothing extra today and it's expensive to go back for later.
- **Day 2 (rest/organize):** No cutting. Confirm folder structure is clean, footage backed up in two places, and do a light re-scan for anything you missed on shoot night. This day exists specifically so Day 3 doesn't start on shoot-day fatigue.
- **Day 3 (triage):** Stage 1, 1–2 hrs. Full selects pass, shortlist built. Stop there — don't let triage bleed into cutting just because you're in the timeline already.
- **Day 4–5 (rough cut):** Stage 2, 2–4 hrs, can split across both days if the deliverable is longer or there are multiple deliverables. This tier is where you're allowed to try a structure, scrap it, and try again — that exploratory slack is the whole point of taking 5-7 days instead of 3.
- **Client check-in (if applicable):** send a rough cut for sign-off before refine, especially on anything with brand/creative-direction risk (Alpha Mode, new clients). Cheaper to redirect structure now than after a full refine pass.
- **Day 6 (refine):** Stage 3, 1–3 hrs. Color, audio, pacing tighten, titles.
- **Day 7 (buffer + delivery):** Hold this day empty on purpose. If everything ran on schedule, use it for Stage 5 export/delivery plus any last-minute revision from the Day 6 client look. If something slipped, this is where it absorbs — not into an all-nighter.

**Why the rest days matter:** the failure mode this whole SOP exists to kill is cramming into shoot-day-adjacent hours. A 5–7 day quote only works as a *system* if the empty days stay empty until they're needed — don't reflexively fill Day 2 or Day 7 with edit work just because you technically could.

### Tier 2 — Compressed (3-day floor)

This is the standard floor already set in the quoting formula — use it whenever the client needs it fast but it's not a live/emergency situation. It is tight but sane, not reckless.

- **Day 1 — shoot + ingest:** Stage 0 same-day ingest as always (30–45 min, evening), **plus** a same-night quick-scan triage of the obvious best 20–30% of footage while it's fresh (add ~20–30 min). This is the one place this tier breaks the "shoot night = ingest only" rule, and only lightly — you're not cutting, just flagging standout clips so Day 2 starts faster. Skip this if the shoot ran past 9pm or you're genuinely depleted; better to lose 20 minutes than start Day 2 exhausted.
- **Day 2 — triage + rough cut:** **4–6 hrs total.** Finish triage in the first 45–60 min (faster than the standard 1–2 hrs because half of it happened Day 1 night), then go straight into rough cut, 3–5 hrs. Cut once, structurally — don't build two versions and pick, pick your structure and commit. This is the single biggest time-saver versus Tier 1: no exploratory cutting.
- **Day 3 — refine + export + deliver:** **2–4 hrs.** One refine pass (color, audio, pacing), one export, deliver. **No scheduled client review gap inside this tier** — you're delivering what you'd otherwise send as a "rough cut for feedback" in Tier 1, polished to final. Tell the client up front: one revision round is included, but it happens *after* delivery, not before, and it adds 1–2 hrs whenever it lands — don't silently absorb a revision round inside Day 3.

**What gets simplified vs. Tier 1, explicitly:**
- One structural pass, not two or three. If it's watchable end-to-end and on-brand, ship it — don't chase a marginally better cut.
- No pre-refine client check-in. Higher trust in your first-cut judgment is required for this tier; if it's a brand-new client with unclear taste, push them toward Tier 1 instead.
- Color/audio refine is a single focused pass, not iterative tweaking across multiple sessions.
- No dedicated buffer day. If Day 2's rough cut runs long, it eats into Day 3's refine time directly — there's no Day 7 equivalent to absorb it. If you're regularly blowing through Day 2, that's a signal to quote Tier 1 next time, not to keep squeezing Tier 2.

### Tier 3 — Live / same-day / next-day (emergency tier)

Use only for genuine rush jobs or event-day turnaround needs (e.g., a client needs a recap video live that evening or the next morning). This is not "Tier 2 but faster" — it's a different method, because there isn't enough time left for a normal triage→cut→refine sequence.

**Method — different in kind, not just speed:**
1. **Shoot-and-cull-as-you-go.** Don't wait for a separate triage pass. Flag/star good takes on the card or in-camera during natural breaks in the shoot itself. By the time you stop shooting, your shortlist already exists.
2. **Edit in camera-order.** Don't reorganize for a "better" structure unless something is glaringly out of place. Camera-order cutting is faster and, for recap/event content, usually reads fine — the audience isn't judging structure, they're judging speed and presence.
3. **Pre-built templates, presets, and title/lower-third graphics prepped *before* the job**, not during. If you know a same-day job is coming (an event booking, a rush client), spend 15–20 min the day before loading a project template with your standard LUT/color preset, audio ducking preset, and title style already in place. This is the highest-leverage prep step in this whole tier — it's the difference between "fast" and "possible."
4. **Minimal color/grade pass.** Apply the pre-built preset, do a global exposure/white-balance check, move on. No shot-by-shot grading.
5. **Deliver a single tight cut. Do not iterate.** One version, sent as final. If the client wants changes, that's a follow-up job on its own timeline — say so explicitly when you deliver ("this is the fast turnaround cut — happy to do a polished revision separately if needed, on a normal timeline").

**Rough time model:** same-day event work — 1–2 hrs cutting immediately post-event, prepped templates already loaded, single export, delivered within hours. Next-day — cull happens the night of (30–45 min beyond normal ingest), 2–3 hrs cutting the following morning, delivered by afternoon.

**Burnout guardrails (non-negotiable):**
- **Maximum 2 consecutive same-day/next-day jobs before a mandatory recovery day off editing entirely.** This tier runs on adrenaline and prepped shortcuts — it is not sustainable back-to-back, and quality silently degrades on the third one even if you don't notice it happening.
- **After any Tier 3 job, the next job you take should default to Tier 1 or Tier 2 — not another Tier 3 — unless there's a specific reason it can't.**
- **When to say no or push back:** if a client asks for same-day/next-day turnaround and (a) you've already run 2 consecutive Tier 3 jobs, (b) the ask arrived with no prep window at all (no template time, no advance warning of the shoot), or (c) the footage volume is heavy enough that even camera-order cutting won't fit the window — say so. A reasonable pushback script: *"I can do a fast single-cut version today if I can prep templates beforehand — if that's not possible, realistic turnaround is [Tier 2 timeline] for something I'd put my name on properly."* Charging a rush premium for Tier 3 jobs is reasonable and should be standard, not apologized for.

### Which tier to quote — quick decision rule

- No external deadline pressure, or a new/detail-sensitive client → **Tier 1.**
- Client needs it fast, relationship/trust is established, footage volume is normal → **Tier 2 (the floor).**
- Live event or genuine same-day/next-day ask → **Tier 3, only if burnout guardrails allow it, with a rush premium.**
- Don't let a client's urgency alone bump you into Tier 3 — Tier 2 with a firm "3 business days" answer is usually enough, and preserves quality and sanity that Tier 3 deliberately trades away.
